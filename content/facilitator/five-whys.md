# Five Whys — for facilitators

Five Whys is a root-cause tool: the group drills from a problem to its
underlying causes by repeatedly asking "why", building a collaborative tree.
You mark the true root causes for downstream work — divergent, but diverging
on *causes* rather than solutions.

## Samvinas

- State the problem clearly in the **Prompt**.
- Let the group branch; use the outliner view to follow the tree as it grows.
- Mark the root causes — they're copied out as items for the next step.

![The Five Whys tree on the display view. The starting question sits at the top, with a counter reading five nodes. Two first-level answers each indent to their own children, one of them two levels deep, so the chain of causes reads as a nested outline.](/assets/images/screenshots/five-whys-tree.png)

The marked root causes are what flows onward — connect the next tool's
**Source** to Five Whys and it works on the causes, not the whole tree.

## Paper and pen

You need: a wall or flip chart, sticky notes, a fat marker, and red dot
stickers.

- Write the problem on a single note at the top of the wall.
- Ask "why does this happen?" — each answer goes on its own note in a row
  beneath. Several answers at once is a branch, not a mistake.
- Pick a note and ask why *that* happens; build each chain downward. Five
  levels is a guide, not a rule — stop when the answers turn structural.
- Mark the root causes with red dots as the group agrees on them.
- The failure mode: stopping at the first comfortable answer — often a
  person's name. Steer from blame toward systems and conditions, and keep
  drilling.
- Harvest: photograph the tree, then copy each red-dotted cause onto a fresh
  note. Those travel to the next activity; the tree stays on the wall.

## When & why

> This is Clarify work — you are still deciding what the problem *is*. See
> [the four stages](/principles/four-stages.html) and
> [framing problems as questions](/principles/problems-as-questions.html).

Reach for it when the group is poised to solve a symptom: the stated problem
keeps recurring, or the obvious fix has been tried before.

- Keep asking "why" past the first comfortable answer; the real cause is
  usually a few levels down.
- Steer away from blame toward systems and conditions.
- What usually follows: reframe each root cause as a *"How might we…?"* and
  take it into [Brainstorm](/facilitator/brainstorm.html); with several
  causes, [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) picks which to attack first.

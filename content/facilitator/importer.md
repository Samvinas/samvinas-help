# Importer — for facilitators

The Importer is where an event's starting material goes. When the ideas
already exist — from a previous session, a survey, a document, a KIStorm
export — bring them in and let the rest of the flow work on them, instead of
asking participants to brainstorm a list you already have.

## Samvinas

- Add **Importer** to your flow, usually as the first tool. It deliberately
  has no way to add ideas on screen — everything arrives through one door,
  the import box.
- It starts **hidden from participants** — that's on purpose. It's a supply
  cupboard, not an activity; participants see its ideas through whichever
  tool you connect to it.
- Open the tool's gear menu, go to the **Data** tab, and tap
  **Import items…**. Paste your list — one idea per line — and import.
- The **Contents** list above the button shows what landed. Tap an item's
  text to fix a typo; the ⋯ menu deletes a stray line.
- One line becomes one idea, so tidy the list in your notes app first — strip
  numbering, headers, and blank padding. Importing again **appends**
  everything you paste, including lines already there; adding more later,
  paste just the new lines and glance at Contents.
- Want participants to see the raw list as a reference page? Flip the tool
  visible in its **Settings** tab. They get a read-only view — looking is
  fine, adding still goes through you.

Connect the next tool (Voting, Clustering, PPCO…) to the Importer as its
source — tools added straight after it connect automatically — and the pasted
ideas flow downstream like any brainstormed ones.

## Paper and pen

The analogue Importer is preparation: the ideas go up before the group
arrives.

You need: the source list, sticky notes or index cards, a fat marker, and
quiet time before the session.

- Write each idea on its own note, big, and **number them** — "number 12" is
  how a group refers to a card it didn't write.
- Post them as a ready-made wall, or deal them into envelopes — one set per
  table — if groups will work separately.
- Introduce the wall with its provenance (*"these came from your survey
  answers"*) and invite the group to work on the cards, not admire them.
- The failure mode: presenting the list as finished. A tidy, pre-printed wall
  reads as decided, and the group critiques instead of building. Handwritten,
  slightly rough cards signal work-in-progress.
- Harvest: as with any wall, photograph it before the next activity
  rearranges everything.

## When & why

Reach for the Importer whenever the starting material predates the event —
survey results, a prior workshop's shortlist, stakeholder interviews. It
spares the group re-generating what already exists and gets them straight to
the work only they can do: judging, clustering, developing.

- Be transparent about provenance; a group works harder on ideas it trusts.
- Seeded ideas are a floor, not a ceiling — pair it with a
  [Brainstorm](/facilitator/brainstorm.html) if you also want fresh thinking.
- What usually follows: almost anything — [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html)
  or [Clustering](/facilitator/clustering.html) to work the list, or
  [PPCO](/facilitator/ppco.html) to develop a single imported proposal.

# PMI — for facilitators

PMI (Plus, Minus, Interesting) is Edward de Bono's quick, balanced scan of an
option. Instead of reacting for or against, the group deliberately looks in
three directions: what's good, what's poor, and what's simply
**interesting** — worth noting even though it's neither good nor bad. An
**organising** tool: it arranges the group's reactions without cutting
anything.

## Samvinas

- Pipe in the options to scan. Each becomes a card with three sections:
  - **Plus** — the good points.
  - **Minus** — the weak or problematic points.
  - **Interesting** — anything noteworthy, curious, or "what if…" about it.
- PMI is fast — use it to open up a considered discussion, not to score a
  winner.

PMI consumes `idea` items. If you need a choice afterwards, pipe onward into
[Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or an
[Evaluation Matrix](/facilitator/evaluation-matrix.html).

## Paper and pen

You need: a flip-chart sheet per option, fat markers, and a timer.

- Rule each sheet into three columns — **Plus**, **Minus**, **Interesting** —
  with the option written across the top.
- Direct everyone's attention the same way at the same time: two minutes
  where the *whole group* offers only Pluses, two of Minuses, two of
  Interestings. Scribe fast; no discussion yet.
- The Interesting column takes the leftovers judgement would discard —
  implications, questions, "I wonder whether…". Prompt for it explicitly.
- Failure mode: letting it slide into a debate or a tally. Nobody argues a
  point during the scan, and nobody counts the columns at the end — it's a
  scan, not a score.
- Keep the sheets on the wall for the discussion that follows; photograph
  them before the group moves on.

## When & why

Reach for PMI when the group is reacting for or against too quickly and you
want a deliberate look in all three directions before the conversation
starts. A few minutes per option is enough.

> The Interesting column is the whole trick: it parks judgement for a moment
> and surfaces what people would otherwise miss — the same move as
> [deferring judgement](/principles/deferring-judgement.html), in miniature.

- Insist on the Interesting column; it's the part that changes minds.
- Keep it quick — momentum matters more than completeness.
- What usually follows: [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or an
  [Evaluation Matrix](/facilitator/evaluation-matrix.html) if you need a
  choice, or [PPCO](/facilitator/ppco.html) to properly develop the option
  the scan favoured.

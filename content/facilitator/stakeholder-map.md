# Stakeholder Map — for facilitators

A variant of [Value / Doability](/facilitator/value-doability.html) for mapping
people and groups on two axes (Influence / Interest by default). An
**organising** tool for engagement and change planning.

## Samvinas

- Pipe in the list of stakeholders (from a Brainstorm of "who's affected?").
- Set the axis labels to fit your framework.

![The Stakeholder Map results view: the same scatter plot with Interest across the bottom and Influence up the side. Six numbered dots spread across the quadrants, coloured blue, amber and orange according to how much the group agreed. A numbered key below names each stakeholder group.](/assets/images/screenshots/stakeholder-map-scatter.png)

## Paper and pen

You need: a flip chart, a fat marker, sticky notes (one stakeholder each), and
a small strip of dot stickers per person.

- Draw a big 2×2: **Interest** across the bottom, **Influence** up the side.
  Write the quadrant names in light pencil if the group knows the framework.
- Write each stakeholder on its own sticky note before any placing starts.
- Place by dots, not by debate: for each stakeholder, everyone puts a dot where
  *they* would place it — then stick the note at the centre of gravity.
- The failure mode is anchoring — the first confident placement drags everyone
  else's. Dots-before-discussion is the cure; scattered dots mark exactly the
  stakeholders worth arguing about.
- Harvest: photograph the finished map, and list each quadrant's stakeholders
  with the agreed engagement move next to them.

## When & why

Reach for the Stakeholder Map when a plan is taking shape and the group needs
to see **whose support it depends on** — it organises the cast of characters
without ranking or cutting anyone.

- Use the quadrants to plan: high influence + high interest → manage closely;
  high influence + low interest → keep satisfied; and so on.
- Differences in placement often reveal different assumptions about who
  matters — surface them; the disagreement is the insight.
- Placements go stale — a map made early in a change effort is worth redrawing
  once the work is underway.
- What usually follows: a [Brainstorm](/facilitator/brainstorm.html) of
  engagement moves for the critical quadrants, then
  [Sign Up](/facilitator/signup.html) to put names against them.

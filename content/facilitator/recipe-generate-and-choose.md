# Generate and choose — the classic shape

This recipe gives you the most dependable session design we know: **Brainstorm → Clustering → Voting**. Reach for it when your group needs to move from an open question to a small set of shared priorities in a single sitting. It fits comfortably in an hour, it works with six people or sixty, and it forgives almost any surprise the room produces. If you only ever learn one design, this is the one to learn.

## When to reach for it

You have a genuinely open question — the kind that starts "How might we…" — and the people in the room hold pieces of the answer. What you need at the end is not just a list of ideas, but a short list the group feels ownership of. That last part is important: it is the difference between a meeting that produces a decision and a meeting that produces a decision people actually carry out. However, if the real difficulty is that nobody agrees on what the problem *is*, pause here — you want to start one step earlier, with a tool like [Five Whys](/facilitator/five-whys.html), and come back to this recipe once the question is clear.

## Setting it up

Create your event, then add three tools to the flow: [Brainstorm](/facilitator/brainstorm.html), [Clustering](/facilitator/clustering.html) and [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html). As you add Clustering and Voting, connect each one to the tool before it — this is what lets the ideas travel through the session without anyone retyping them.

Before the session, write your opening question into the Brainstorm prompt. It is worth spending real time on this sentence, because the quality of everything that follows depends on it. A good question is open but bounded: "How might we welcome new staff in their first week?" will serve you far better than either "How can we improve onboarding?" (too broad) or "Should we assign buddies?" (already closed).

## Running it

**Brainstorm — about 20 minutes.** Set the tool to Focus and let the ideas come. Your job during this phase is to keep judgment out of the room: every idea is welcome, including the odd ones — in fact, especially the odd ones, because they are often the seed of something genuinely new. If the flow slows, don't rush to rescue it; a quiet minute usually precedes a second wave.

**Clustering — about 15 minutes.** Move Focus to Clustering, and invite the group to gather related ideas together. One key point we need to highlight: the aim here is for the room to *see its own thinking*, not to produce a tidy taxonomy. Two or three untidy clusters that spark conversation are worth more than eight neat ones that don't. Name each cluster as it forms — the names often turn out to be the real ideas.

**Voting — about 10 minutes.** Move Focus to Voting and give everyone a small number of votes; three to five works well. Fewer votes means each one carries genuine weight. When the votes are in, show the results and — this matters — talk about them. The vote is the start of the decision, not the end of it.

**Closing — the last 5 minutes.** Say out loud what was chosen, and agree what happens next: who takes each priority forward, and by when. A session that ends without this step produces a warm feeling and nothing else.

## Ways to vary it

The shape bends to the room. If a few loud voices are likely to dominate, swap Brainstorm for [Brain Writing](/facilitator/brainwriting.html) — everyone writes before anyone speaks. If the stakes of the outcome are high, follow the vote with [PPCO](/facilitator/ppco.html) to strengthen the winning idea rather than merely selecting it. And if the group is meeting in a room with a good wall, remember that the middle step runs beautifully on sticky notes — the tool pages' *Paper and pen* tabs show you how, and the Importer will bring the results back into Samvinas afterwards.

Facilitating this well is not complicated, but the difference between adequate and excellent lies almost entirely in the question you open with and the clarity you close with. Give those two moments your preparation time, and the middle will largely take care of itself.

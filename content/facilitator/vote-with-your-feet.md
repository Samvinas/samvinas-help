# Vote With Your Feet — for facilitators

Vote With Your Feet is the digital version of asking people to stand somewhere
along a line across the room. It's a **warm-up** that makes the spread of
opinion visible. You give a statement, everyone places themselves between two
ends, and then you reveal where people stand.

## Samvinas

- Add it from the **Warm-ups** section of the toolbox. Each step holds **one
  statement**, so for a series of statements add a step for each.
- Put the statement in the step's **Prompt** (Basics tab). On the **Settings**
  tab, label the two ends of the line (by default *Strongly disagree* and
  *Strongly agree*).
- **Names:** *Show names at the reveal* is on by default, and participants are
  asked for their name once per event. Turn it off for a sensitive statement:
  nobody is asked for a name, and the reveal shows anonymous dots.
- **Roles (optional):** list up to eight roles, one per line (e.g. Funder,
  Researcher, Practitioner). Each role gets its own colour, and at the reveal you
  can show one role at a time.
- **Focus the step to open it.** Participants move a slider to place
  themselves, and their position saves as they move. While voting is open the
  projector shows anonymous dots, so nobody is swayed by who stands where.
- Press **Close and reveal** on the step's row in the flow list (it also shows
  how many people have placed themselves). The dots become names, in role
  colours.
- **Reopen** and **Run again** are on the step's **Data** tab, along with a table
  of every name, role and position. The event report and CSV list everyone in
  order along the line.

## Paper and pen

You need: a long strip of masking tape across the floor, and a sign for each
end of the line.

- Read the statement aloud and point out the two ends.
- Ask everyone to decide where they stand **before** anyone moves, then move
  together. Otherwise people drift towards where their friends are.
- Once people are placed, ask two or three people at different points to say
  why they are standing there. The ends are the obvious choices, but the
  middle often has the most interesting reasons.
- The failure mode is a crowd in the middle. Push the statement harder, or ask
  people to "lean one way or the other" before they settle.
- Harvest: photograph the line from one end, and note the reasons you heard.

## When & why

Reach for Vote With Your Feet when you want to show a group how differently its
members see something, before you start working on it.

- **Write a statement people can disagree with.** "Our customers are happy"
  splits a room; "Customers matter" doesn't.
- **The reveal starts the conversation.** Invite people from different points
  to explain where they stand, and listen for the assumptions behind the
  positions.
- **Roles show patterns.** With roles set, showing one role at a time can
  reveal that the funders and the practitioners stand in quite different
  places, which is worth discussing openly.
- **Protect people on sensitive topics.** Turn names off when standing visibly
  in one place could cost someone something.
- What usually follows: discussion, then a clarifying or generating tool such
  as [Five Whys](/facilitator/five-whys.html) or
  [Brainstorm](/facilitator/brainstorm.html) on the question the split has
  raised.

# Brain Writing — for facilitators

Brain Writing generates ideas silently and in parallel: each participant builds
on provocations drawn from others' ideas. A **divergent** tool — like Brainstorm
without the talking.

## Samvinas

- Give a clear, open **Prompt**, as you would for a Brainstorm.
- Seed it with a stimulus — or pipe in starting ideas from an earlier tool —
  so the first screens have something to build on.
- Then give people a little time; the rounds do the rest. Each participant is
  handed others' ideas as springboards, so the thinking cross-pollinates
  without anyone speaking.

Brain Writing produces `idea` items, and can consume them as seed stock —
connect its **Source** to an earlier tool to start from existing material, and
pipe its harvest onward like any brainstorm's.

## Paper and pen

You need: one A4 sheet per person ruled into a grid — three columns, five or
six rows — a pen each, and a timer.

- Write the prompt where everyone can see it. Each person writes **three ideas
  in the top row** — five minutes, in silence.
- On the bell, everyone passes their sheet to the left. Read the ideas above,
  then add three more in the next row — build on, twist, or combine what's
  there.
- Repeat until the sheets are full; five or six rounds with six people yields
  a hundred-odd ideas.
- The failure mode: people ignoring the sheet and starting from scratch each
  round. Say it up front — *react to what you've been handed*.
- Harvest: read the best aloud, or cut the sheets into strips and number them
  so they can be dot-voted or clustered next.

## When & why

Reach for Brain Writing when a spoken [Brainstorm](/facilitator/brainstorm.html)
would be lopsided — a few voices dominate, the topic is sensitive, or the group
simply thinks better in writing.

> This is a [divergent](/principles/divergence.html) step: quantity first,
> judgement later.

- Silence is the feature, not a limitation — quieter voices contribute on
  equal terms.
- The builds are the point: ideas that have passed through three hands are
  usually stranger and better than first thoughts.
- What usually follows: [Clustering](/facilitator/clustering.html) to make
  sense of the harvest, or [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) to converge on it.

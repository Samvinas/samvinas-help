# ALoU — for facilitators

ALoU is a way to weigh up a promising option in a constructive, balanced way:
its **Advantages**, its **Limitations**, and its **Unique Features** — then
the group works to **overcome** the most important limitations rather than
letting them sink the idea. An **organising** tool, it runs on the same
engine as [PPCO](/facilitator/ppco.html); the difference is the lens.

## Samvinas

- Pipe in the ideas you want to develop (for example, the winners of a vote).
  Each idea becomes a card.
- Participants open a card and add to three sections:
  - **Advantages** — what is genuinely strong about it.
  - **Limitations** — stated as questions ("How might we…?") so they can be
    answered.
  - **Unique Features** — what is original or distinctive here.
- Under each limitation, the group adds **Overcomes** — concrete ways to
  answer that specific limitation.

ALoU consumes `idea` items; developed ideas flow on — pipe it into
[Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html)
to re-choose after the analysis.

## Paper and pen

You need: a flip-chart sheet per option, fat markers, sticky notes, and dot
stickers.

- Rule each sheet into three columns — **Advantages**, **Limitations**,
  **Unique Features** — with the option written across the top.
- Fill the columns in order, one sticky note per point. Limitations go up
  only as questions; rewrite any flat verdict before posting it.
- Dot-mark the limitations that actually matter, then answer those:
  overcome stickies go directly under the limitation they address.
- Failure mode: skipping Unique Features because the column is slow to fill.
  Ask directly — "what does *only this option* give us?" — and wait.
- Harvest: photograph each sheet and carry the option forward with its
  unique features and answered limitations written on one fresh card.

## When & why

Reach for ALoU in the **develop** stage, when you want people to appreciate
what is *distinctive* about an option — not only its pluses and problems.
The Unique Features step is what separates it from a plain pros-and-cons
list, and it often surfaces the real reason an idea is worth pursuing.

> Judgement stays affirmative: strengths first, limitations as questions.
> The reasoning is on [convergence](/principles/convergence.html).

- Insist that limitations are phrased as questions. "It's too expensive"
  stops the conversation; "How might we bring the cost down?" invites an
  overcome.
- You don't need to overcome every limitation — focus on the ones that
  matter.
- What usually follows: [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or
  [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html) to choose with clearer eyes, or
  [Elaboration](/facilitator/elaboration.html) to build out the winner.

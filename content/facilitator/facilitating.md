# Facilitating the room

Six habits that carry most of the weight — in a Samvinas event or any meeting
at all. Skim the bold lines; the rest is there when you want the reasoning.

## Guard the process; stay out of the content

The problem belongs to the group. The way of working belongs to you.

The moment you champion an idea — or even reword one — you have quietly become
a judge, and deferred judgement stops being credible. If an idea of yours won't
let go: say out loud that you're stepping out of the facilitator role, offer it
once without selling it, and step back in.

## Guide by the side, not sage on the stage

The group doesn't need your answers. It needs your structure.

Ask the question, set up the activity, let the room think. Conclusions a group
reaches itself are the only ones it keeps. (The rhyme is owed to the educator
Alison King.)

## Neutral doesn't mean passive

Staying out of the content is not fading into the wallpaper.

Run the room actively on the group's behalf: protect the quiet, connect the
strangers, check status at the door. In here, the intern's idea and the
director's idea weigh the same. Clear rules, said out loud, make people freer —
not less free.

## Make participation full, not merely open

"Anyone can speak" quietly becomes "the confident speak."

In open discussion only one person talks at a time, and everyone else's
thinking stalls while they wait — researchers call this **production
blocking**, and it is exactly why every Samvinas generating tool lets the whole
room write at once. Start topics with silent writing, invite quieter people by
name (with a free pass to decline), and when one view is rolling, ask *"who
sees it differently?"*

## Respect different ways of working

Some people talk in order to think; others think before they will talk.

Some light up generating options; others come alive clarifying, or planning.
Alternate solo, pair and whole-group moments. Share the challenge ahead of the
day so reflectors arrive ready. Hold a few seconds of quiet after a question —
silence is thinking, not failure.

## Keep the ground rules, and the group's words

You are the keeper of
[deferring judgement](/principles/deferring-judgement.html) while the field is
open, and of [affirmative-first choosing](/principles/convergence.html) when it
narrows.

Capture ideas in the contributor's own words. Rewording is judgement in
disguise. If an idea runs long, ask its owner for the headline rather than
writing one for them.

# Group Sort — for facilitators

Group Sort asks everyone to sort the same ideas into groups **on their own**,
then combines the sorts to show which groups the room agrees on. It's an
**organising** tool, like Clustering, but the grouping is done privately
rather than in conversation. If you have used Well Sorted, this is the same
method.

## Samvinas

- Pipe in the ideas (e.g. from Brainstorm). The first line of each idea becomes
  the card's title, and anything after it is shown as a description.
- **Before a meeting:** make the step visible and share the event link. With
  nothing Focused, people open Group Sort from the menu and sort when they have
  time. **Live:** Focus the step.
- Each person sorts the cards into as many groups as they like. On a phone they
  see one card at a time; on a laptop they see the whole pile beside the board.
  Sorting works with a tap, a click, the keyboard or a screen reader, and
  dragging is optional.
- **Every saved sort counts, finished or not.** Someone who places 20 of 34
  cards and runs out of time still contributes those 20. Their work saves as
  they go, so nobody needs to press *I'm done*.
- Sorts are anonymous, and the projector shows only how many have come in until
  you finalise, so nobody is steered by the room.
- Open the step's **Data** tab to watch the sorts arrive and preview the
  groups. Choose the **number of groups** (2–10). Greyed-out numbers are ones
  the sorts can't produce. When the preview looks right, press **Finalise**.
- Then **name the groups** on the Data tab. If you set *Everyone can:* **Edit**
  on the Settings tab, participants can name them too.
- Once the groups are final, the projector has four views: the named
  **Groups**, the **Tree** showing how the ideas joined, a **Similarity** map
  with the most-agreed pairs, and **Ideas by group**. The Tree and the
  Similarity map are sized to fit the screen, each in a white card with its
  explanation; a very long tree scrolls instead of shrinking past a readable
  size. The event report and the CSV export carry the same results.
- **Later steps receive the groups by name.** Pipe Group Sort into Voting to
  vote on the themes. Nothing passes on until you finalise, and renaming a group
  afterwards keeps any votes on it. **Reopen** takes the groups back out of
  later steps, so do it before anyone votes.

## Paper and pen

You need: one envelope per person holding a numbered set of cards (one idea per
card, 15–40 cards), paper clips, and a recording sheet.

- Ask each person to sort their deck **alone and in silence** into piles of
  ideas that belong together. Allow as many piles as they like, and allow a
  single card to sit on its own.
- Have them clip each pile and write the card numbers of each pile on the
  recording sheet. That sheet is the data.
- Tally which numbers share a pile across the sheets. Pairs that most people
  put together form the core of each group. With more than a dozen sheets, type
  them into a spreadsheet rather than counting by hand.
- The failure mode is a deck that's too big. Past about 40 cards people tire and
  start making one large "miscellaneous" pile, which tells you nothing.
- Harvest: the recording sheets, plus a flip chart of the agreed groups once
  the room has named them.

## When & why

Reach for Group Sort when you want the room's **independent** view of how the
ideas fall together. Clustering is social, and the first confident voice
often decides the shape. In Group Sort, everyone's judgement carries the same
weight.

- **It saves meeting time.** Run it before the session and arrive with the
  groups already formed, ready to name and discuss.
- **Agreement is information.** A tight group is a theme people already share.
  A loose one deserves a conversation, and an idea that floats between groups is
  often the interesting one.
- **Naming is a group activity.** Look at each group together and give it a name
  that says what it is about. A name that *does* something, such as a verb phrase
  or a fresh "How might we…?", turns a pile into a proposal.
- About 15–40 ideas works best. For fewer, Clustering in the room is quicker.
  For more, narrow the list first with [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html).
- What usually follows: [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or
  [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html) on the named groups. To compare with the
  social version, see [Clustering](/facilitator/clustering.html).

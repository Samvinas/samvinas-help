# Group Sort — for facilitators

Group Sort turns a long list of ideas into a handful of named groups. It's
Samvinas's **organising** tool, and it works in two ways. Choose one on the
step's **Settings** tab, under *How people sort*:

- **Together, on one shared board.** Everyone watches one board while the
  ideas are moved into groups, either by you or by the whole room. This is the
  classic affinity sort, and it replaces the old Clustering tool.
- **Everyone on their own, then combine the sorts.** Each person sorts the same
  ideas privately, and Samvinas combines the sorts to show which groups the
  room agrees on. If you have used Well Sorted, this is the same method.

## Samvinas: sorting together

- Pipe in the ideas (e.g. from Brainstorm). Each idea becomes a card on the
  board, in a pile marked *Not grouped yet*.
- **Who moves the cards** is set by *Everyone can:* on the Settings tab. At
  **View**, only you move cards and everyone else watches the board change
  live. At **Add**, everyone can move cards from their own device. When you
  first switch a step to sorting together, it moves to **View**.
- On the step's **Data** tab, press **Run the board ↗**. The board opens full
  screen in its own tab. Project that tab and drag the cards, or pick a card and
  choose *Put it here*. Every screen flashes the group a card has just gone into.
- Every group has a name from the start ("Group 3"). Click a name to rename it.
  Participants at **Add** can rename the groups they made; at **Edit** they can
  rename any group.
- **Already sorted on your own?** If you switch a step from *on their own* to
  *together* after finalising the groups, the board starts from those groups and
  their names. The room can then refine them together.
- **Later steps receive the groups by name, plus any cards still in the pile.**
  Pipe the step into Voting to vote on the themes.
- **Clear the board…** on the Data tab puts every card back in the pile and
  deletes the groups.

## Samvinas: everyone on their own

- Pipe in the ideas (e.g. from Brainstorm). The first line of each idea becomes
  the card's title, and anything after it is shown as a description.
- **Before a meeting:** make the step visible and share the event link. With
  nothing Focused, people open Group Sort from the menu and sort when they have
  time. **Live:** Focus the step.
- Each person sorts the cards into as many groups as they like. On a phone they
  see one card at a time; on a laptop they see the whole pile beside the board.
  Sorting works with a tap, a click, the keyboard or a screen reader, and
  dragging is optional.
- **Every saved sort counts, finished or not.** Someone who places 20 of 34
  cards and runs out of time still contributes those 20. Their work saves as
  they go, so nobody needs to press *I'm done*.
- Sorts are anonymous, and the projector shows only how many have come in until
  you finalise, so nobody is steered by the room.
- Open the step's **Data** tab to watch the sorts arrive and preview the
  groups. Choose the **number of groups** (2–10). Greyed-out numbers are ones
  the sorts can't produce. When the preview looks right, press **Finalise**.
- Then **name the groups** on the Data tab. If you set *Everyone can:* **Edit**
  on the Settings tab, participants can name them too.
- Once the groups are final, the projector has four views: the named
  **Groups**, the **Tree** showing how the ideas joined, a **Similarity** map
  with the most-agreed pairs, and **Ideas by group**. The Tree and the
  Similarity map are sized to fit the screen, each in a white card with its
  explanation; a very long tree scrolls instead of shrinking past a readable
  size. The event report and the CSV export carry the same results.
- **Later steps receive the groups by name.** Pipe Group Sort into Voting to
  vote on the themes. Nothing passes on until you finalise, and renaming a group
  afterwards keeps any votes on it. **Reopen** takes the groups back out of
  later steps, so do it before anyone votes.

## Samvinas AI

If your organisation has **Samvinas AI** switched on, the step's **Data** tab
offers AI help. Only you, the facilitator, can ask for it. The AI sees the text
of the ideas, never who wrote them.

- **Sorting together: ✨ Let AI make a first sort.** The AI sorts the cards
  still in the pile into named groups, and they appear straight away on the
  board everyone is watching. Then move cards, rename groups or add new ones as
  the room sees fit. **Undo AI sort** takes the AI's groups off again and puts
  their cards back in the pile. It leaves alone any group someone has already
  renamed or changed.
- **On their own: ✨ Add the AI's sort.** The AI sorts the ideas once, and its
  sort counts exactly like one person's. Wherever the sorts are counted it's
  labelled ("3 people + AI"), so the room knows. To remove it, open **Sorts** on
  the Data tab and choose **Withdraw** next to *The AI's sort*. Asking again
  replaces it.

Samvinas AI is switched on per organisation, and is off unless your
organisation has arranged it. Without it, the AI buttons don't appear.

## Paper and pen

**Together:** you need the sticky-note wall from the previous activity, a few
stickies in a second colour for group names, and fat markers.

- Start with a few minutes of **silent sorting**: everyone moves notes so that
  related ideas sit together. No talking yet, because silence stops one voice
  pre-sorting the wall for the room.
- Then talk: walk the emerging clumps, split or merge where the group
  disagrees, and leave loners alone. An ungrouped corner is fine.
- Name each group on a different-coloured sticky placed at its top, so names
  read at a glance from across the room.
- Harvest: photograph each named group close enough to read, then the whole
  wall.

**On their own:** you need one envelope per person holding a numbered set of
cards (one idea per card, 15–40 cards), paper clips, and a recording sheet.

- Ask each person to sort their deck **alone and in silence** into piles of
  ideas that belong together. Allow as many piles as they like, and allow a
  single card to sit on its own.
- Have them clip each pile and write the card numbers of each pile on the
  recording sheet. That sheet is the data.
- Tally which numbers share a pile across the sheets. Pairs that most people
  put together form the core of each group. With more than a dozen sheets, type
  them into a spreadsheet rather than counting by hand.
- Harvest: the recording sheets, plus a flip chart of the agreed groups once
  the room has named them.

In both, the failure mode is a "miscellaneous" pile that swallows everything.
If a group can't be named, it isn't one yet.

## When & why

Reach for Group Sort when the list has grown past what the group can hold in
mind. It arranges without cutting, so nothing is lost while the shape emerges.

> Group Sort organises the field; it doesn't choose from it. If you want the
> group to *choose*, follow it with a focusing tool. See
> [convergence](/principles/convergence.html).

- **Together** is quick and social, and suits a small group or a short list.
  The first confident voice often decides the shape, so invite quieter people to
  propose groupings, or start from a silent sort.
- **On their own** gives the room's **independent** view: everyone's judgement
  carries the same weight. Run it before the session to save meeting time and
  arrive with the groups already formed. Agreement is information too: a tight
  group is a theme people already share, and an idea that floats between groups
  is often the interesting one. About 15–40 ideas works best.
- You can combine them: sort on their own first, finalise, then switch to
  together and refine the groups with the room.
- Don't over-group: 4–7 groups is usually more useful than fifteen.
- **Naming is a group activity.** A name that *does* something, such as a verb
  phrase ("pilot the scheme with two teams") or a fresh "How might we…?", turns
  a pile into a proposal. One-word labels waste the work the group just did.
- What usually follows: [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or
  [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html) on the named groups, or
  [Value / Doability](/facilitator/value-doability.html) to weigh them.

# Clustering — for facilitators

Clustering makes sense of a big idea list by grouping related items into named
themes. It's an **organising** tool — a natural bridge from divergence to
convergence.

## Samvinas

- Pipe in the ideas (e.g. from Brainstorm).
- Let the group select and group items; encourage clear names.
- Groups collapse by default — expand to review them.

![The Clustering view: two named groups, "People and support" and "Ways of working", each showing a count. The first is open, listing the three ideas inside it; the second is still collapsed. Below them an UNGROUPED section holds the two ideas nobody has placed yet.](/assets/images/screenshots/clustering-groups.png)

Clusters are category items; grouped items keep their link back to the original
idea, so downstream tools can work with either the themes or the underlying
ideas — you can vote on or score whole themes.

## Paper and pen

You need: the sticky-note wall from the previous activity, a few stickies in a
second colour for group names, and fat markers.

- Start with a few minutes of **silent sorting**: everyone moves notes so that
  related ideas sit together. No talking yet — silence stops one voice
  pre-sorting the wall for the room.
- Then talk: walk the emerging clumps, split or merge where the group disagrees,
  and leave loners alone — an UNGROUPED corner is fine.
- Name each cluster on a different-coloured sticky placed at its top, so names
  read at a glance from across the room.
- The failure mode is a "miscellaneous" pile that swallows the wall — if a
  cluster can't be named, it isn't one yet.
- Harvest: photograph each named cluster close enough to read, then the whole
  wall. The next activity will want the themes, and the photos keep the detail.

## When & why

Reach for Clustering when the list has grown past what the group can hold in
mind — it arranges without cutting, so nothing is lost while the shape emerges.

> Clustering organises the field; it doesn't choose from it. If you want the
> group to *choose*, follow it with a focusing tool — see
> [convergence](/principles/convergence.html).

- Clustering is social, and a few people often lead. Invite quieter voices to
  propose groupings.
- Don't over-cluster — 4–7 themes is usually more useful than fifteen.
- **Restate each cluster before moving on.** A name that *does* something — a
  verb phrase ("pilot the scheme with two teams"), or a fresh "How might
  we…?" if you're clarifying — turns a pile into a proposal. One-word labels
  waste the work the group just did.
- What usually follows: [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or
  [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html) to converge on themes, or
  [Value / Doability](/facilitator/value-doability.html) to weigh them.

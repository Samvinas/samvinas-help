# Brainstorm — for facilitators

Brainstorm is your idea‑gathering tool: everyone contributes to a shared, live
list at once. It's usually the opening, **divergent** step of a flow.

## Samvinas

- Set a clear, open **Prompt** — the stem depends on where you are in the
  process: *"I wish…"* / *"It would be great if…"* for surfacing goals early
  on; *"How might we…?"* / *"In what ways might we…?"* for generating ideas
  against a chosen problem; *"What might be all the…?"* to widen a hunt.
- Keep it visible and unlocked while ideas flow. Encourage quantity out loud.
- Watch the list; if it stalls, offer a fresh angle or a provocation.
- When you're ready to converge, **Lock** it and pipe the ideas into the next
  tool (e.g. Voting, Clustering, or Value/Doability).

Brainstorm produces `idea` items. Any downstream tool can consume them — add the
next tool and connect its **Source** to this one, optionally filtering by votes
or sequence range.

## Paper and pen

You need: sticky notes, a fat marker per person, and a wall or flip chart.

- Write the prompt where everyone can see it, in the agreed question form.
- **One idea per sticky note**, written big — fat pens force short ideas, and
  short ideas can be read from across the room.
- Say it as you stick it: each person calls their idea out loud while posting
  it, so others can hitch‑hike on it.
- Keep the wall open well past the first lull — restate the prompt or offer a
  provocation to restart the flow.
- Before anything moves: number the notes or photograph the wall. The next
  activity (dot‑voting, clustering by hand) will rearrange everything.

## When & why

Reach for Brainstorm when the group needs **many options before choosing any** —
wishes and goals early in the process, ideas once a problem is framed, possible
actions near the end.

> While the field is open, [defer judgement](/principles/deferring-judgement.html) —
> evaluation is postponed, not abandoned, and it belongs in a focusing tool later.

- Quantity breeds quality: a strong session can reach forty ideas — and the
  *second* forty is where the novelty lives. When good ideas start appearing,
  don't stop: that's the signal to stretch, not to settle.
- Silence is fine; give people time to think. A minute of quiet often produces
  the best ideas.
- If a few voices dominate, [Brain Writing](/facilitator/brainwriting.html) or
  [Breakout Groups](/facilitator/breakout-groups.html) spread participation.
- What usually follows: a focusing tool ([Voting](/facilitator/voting.html),
  [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html)) to converge, or
  [Clustering](/facilitator/clustering.html) to make sense of the field first.

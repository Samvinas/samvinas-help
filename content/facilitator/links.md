# Links — for facilitators

Links is a shared reading list: everyone adds web pages, each with a short
title, and the group sees one tidy list of titles. Every link opens in a new
tab, so nobody loses their place in the session.

## Samvinas

- There are no settings. Add the step, give it a clear **Prompt** (*"What
  have you read that we should all see?"*), and open it.
- People type a **title** and a **web address**. A missing `https://` is
  added for them; anything that isn't a real web page is refused with a
  message.
- Leave the title blank and the site's name is used. Ask for titles that say
  why the page matters — they are all the group sees until someone clicks.
- Set it to **View** when you want the list fixed, for example before a
  reading break.
- People can delete their own links; you can delete any of them from the
  **⋯** menu.

Links produces `idea` items: the title is the text, and the address travels
with it. Connect a [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or
[Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html) step's **Source** to it to choose which
pages to read first.

In a 3D world, the Links poster lists the titles, and tapping a title opens
that page directly.

In an encrypted session, the web addresses are encrypted along with the
titles. The export lists each title with its address.

## Paper and pen

You need: index cards and a wall, or a shared page on a flip chart.

- One card per resource: a short title written big, and the address (or
  "search for…") underneath.
- Afterwards, type the list up and send it round. Paper links can't be
  clicked, so this step matters more than it seems.

## When & why

Reach for Links when the group's knowledge lives in other places: articles,
reports, data sets, earlier work.

- **Before a session:** open it early as pre-reading, so people arrive with
  the same background.
- **During research or exploring the data:** pool what everyone has found
  instead of each person searching alone.
- **At the end:** collect the resources people want to follow up on.

> Links gathers material; it doesn't judge it. If the list grows long, follow
> it with a focusing tool so the group agrees what to read first.

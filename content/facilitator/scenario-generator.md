# Scenario Generator — for facilitators

The Scenario Generator builds scenarios by combination. You name a few
headings, such as *Character*, *Location*, *Objective* and *Challenge*. The
group brainstorms ideas under each heading, and the tool then draws one idea
from every heading to make a scenario. The method is called morphological
analysis. It is a **generating** tool, and it is good at producing
combinations that nobody in the room would have chosen on purpose.

## Samvinas

- **Name your headings** on the tool's **Settings** tab, in the *Dimensions*
  box. Write one per line, and add a question after a colon:
  `Character: Who is the character?` You can have up to eight. If you leave
  the box alone you get Character, Location, Objective and Challenge.
- **Set the headings before people start.** You can reword a heading or its
  question at any time and its ideas stay with it. Reordering the lines, or
  removing one, moves ideas to a different heading.
- **Collecting ideas.** Participants see the list of headings, each with a
  count and a **Brainstorm** button. The button opens that heading's own page,
  with its question, a box for adding ideas and everyone's ideas so far.
- **Generating scenarios.** When the lists are full enough, open the gear,
  choose **Settings** and change *Stage* to **Generate scenarios**.
  Participants move across within a couple of seconds, wherever they are in
  the tool, and so does the projector.
- Each participant now has a **New scenario** button. It draws one idea from
  every heading and adds a card. The draw favours ideas that have been used
  least, so the room's scenarios spread across everything people wrote before
  anything repeats. A heading with no ideas is left off the card.
- **Everyone sees every card**, with their own at the top. Under each card is
  a box for writing what happens in that scenario. Enter starts a new line;
  the **Add** button, or Ctrl+Enter, sends it.
- A card keeps its own copy of the words. If someone edits or deletes an idea
  later, the scenarios already made from it don't change.
- **To add more ideas after generating**, change *Stage* back to
  **Collect ideas**. The scenarios are kept, and they reappear when you
  switch forward again.
- **The projector** shows the ideas in a column per heading while you are
  collecting, and the scenario cards with their responses once you are
  generating.
- **The Data tab** shows you every idea and every scenario. You can edit or
  delete anything there from the ⋯ menu.
- **Later steps receive the scenarios**, each as one line
  (*Character: … · Location: …*). Pipe the Scenario Generator into
  [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) or [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html)
  to choose which scenarios to develop. The raw ideas and the responses stay
  behind.
- **In breakout groups**, add the tool to the group template and set its
  headings there. A group has no facilitator to change the stage, so each
  person gets an **Ideas / Scenarios** switch at the top of the tool and moves
  across when they are ready. The *Stage* setting decides which side opens
  first.
- The event report and the CSV export list the ideas under each heading, then
  each scenario with its responses.

## Paper and pen

You need: a wall or a long table, one flip-chart sheet per heading, sticky
notes and pens for everyone, and one die (or a bag of numbered slips) per
group.

- Head each sheet with one heading and its question, written big. Hang them
  side by side so they read as columns.
- Brainstorm one column at a time, one idea per sticky note. Give each column
  three or four minutes, then move on, so that every column fills.
- Number the notes in each column from the top.
- To make a scenario, roll the die once for each column and read out the
  note with that number. Write the combination on a fresh sheet or an index
  card. If a column has more notes than the die has faces, draw numbered slips
  from a bag instead.
- Give each small group two or three combinations and five minutes with each:
  what happens in this scenario, and what would make it work?
- The failure mode is choosing instead of drawing. People reach for the
  combination that already makes sense to them, and the method only earns its
  keep when chance forces a pairing they would not have picked.
- Harvest: photograph the columns, and keep the scenario cards with the
  group's notes on the back.

## When & why

Reach for the Scenario Generator when the thing you are designing has a few
separate parts, and you want to look at combinations of them that habit would
never suggest. Stories, characters, products, services and future scenarios
all have this shape.

- **Three to five headings is the sweet spot.** With two there is little to
  combine. Beyond six, the cards get long and people stop reading them.
- **Choose headings that are independent of each other.** If the choice in
  one heading settles the choice in another, merge them.
- **Fill every list before you switch.** A list with two ideas in it makes
  every scenario feel the same. Aim for eight or more under each heading.
- **Collecting is still a brainstorm**, so
  [defer judgement](/principles/deferring-judgement.html) and go for quantity.
  The odd idea is often the one that makes a scenario interesting.
- **A strange combination is a constraint, and constraints help.** Ask the
  group to make each scenario work, not to say whether it is a good one.
  Judging comes later.
- What usually follows: [Voting](/facilitator/voting.html) to pick the
  scenarios worth developing, then [PPCo](/facilitator/ppco.html) or
  [Elaboration](/facilitator/elaboration.html) to build them out.

# Convergence

Convergence is the narrowing move: taking the field of options a group has
generated and choosing what to carry forward. It is not the opposite of
creativity — it is the half that turns creativity into outcomes. Done badly,
it undoes the divergence that preceded it; done well, it leaves the group
confident in its choices *and* glad it generated wildly.

## The four guidelines

1. **Use affirmative judgment.** Start from what is promising rather than
   what is wrong. Screening for fatal flaws gets its turn later;
   strengthening comes first — which is what [PPCO](/facilitator/ppco.html)
   exists for.
2. **Check objectives.** Keep the goal in view: does this option serve what
   you set out to do? Choose against explicit criteria — voting, ranking,
   scoring — rather than by whoever talks longest.
3. **Seek novelty.** Novelty is exactly what you diverged for; don't let
   "unusual" be read as "unworkable", or let the familiar option win by
   default.
4. **Improve on options.** Strengthen promising options before choosing
   between them; a flaw is a problem to solve, not a reason to drop the idea.

And don't converge too early. It's the commonest failure: the group grabs
the first workable idea and stops. If the list still feels obvious, diverge
again.

## Highlighting: hits, clusters, restatements

The workhorse of convergence is a small three-step move called highlighting:

1. **Mark the hits.** Sweep the list and mark what stands out. The criteria
   are deliberately gut-level — it's interesting, it's useful, it gets at
   the problem, or it simply keeps pulling your eye back. Trust that pull;
   analysis gets its turn later.
2. **Cluster the related hits.** Group the hits that point the same way —
   [Clustering](/facilitator/clustering.html) makes this a shared activity.
3. **Restate each cluster.** Give every cluster a name that *does*
   something: a verb phrase ("pilot the scheme with two teams"), or — when
   you're clarifying — a fresh
   ["How might we…?"](/principles/problems-as-questions.html) question. A
   restated cluster is a decision taking shape; an unnamed pile is just
   tidiness.

## What it looks like in Samvinas

The **[focusing tools](/facilitator/tools-focusing.html)** —
[Voting](/facilitator/voting.html), [Ranking](/facilitator/ranking.html),
[Value / Doability](/facilitator/value-doability.html), the
[Evaluation Matrix](/facilitator/evaluation-matrix.html) — exist to make
converging visible and shared, rather than something that happens in the
loudest corner of the room.

And convergence only has something to work on because
**[divergence](/principles/divergence.html)** came first.

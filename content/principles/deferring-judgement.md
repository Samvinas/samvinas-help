# Deferring judgement

If creative work has one habit that matters more than the rest, this is it:
while options are being generated, **evaluation waits**. Not forever —
judgement gets its own turn, and better material to work on when it comes —
but later, and everyone in the room knows it.

## Why it matters

Judgement is a tax on contribution. When ideas are evaluated as they arrive,
every person runs their next thought past an internal censor first — and the
censor is conservative. What the group gets is safe, similar ideas, or
silence.

Early ideas are also scaffolding. Many good ideas are only reachable *via* a
mediocre one that someone floated without embarrassment; kill the mediocre
idea on arrival and the good one never gets built. And "that won't work" is
usually premature in any case — at the generating stage, nobody has tried to
make it work yet. That is what the develop stage
([PPCO](/facilitator/ppco.html) and friends) is for.

## How to actually do it

Separate the phases visibly — *now we generate, later we choose* — and mark
the boundary; a shared [timer](/facilitator/timer.html) helps. Watch for the
polite forms of judgement too: "interesting, but…", the raised eyebrow, the
instant feasibility question. They all count. And when evaluation does
arrive, make it affirmative —
[convergence](/principles/convergence.html) starts from what is promising.

Deferring judgement is one half of a pair. The other is
[framing problems as questions](/principles/problems-as-questions.html) —
the habit that keeps judgement constructive when it *is* time to judge.
